Query Preprocessing Modernization

An architectural update to how ChartSearchAI queries are preprocessed before semantic search in the OpenMRS QueryStore. We have retired legacy stopword removal to properly support modern dense embedding models and global multilingual deployments.

Why We Removed Stopword Stripping

Historically, search systems used "stopword" lists to remove common words (like "the", "not", "is") to prevent them from overwhelming keyword search scores. Modern clinical AI pipelines require a different approach.

Grammatical Context is Critical for Transformers

  • Modern dense embedding models (like multilingual-e5) rely on self-attention mechanisms to understand text. They compute the meaning of a word based on the words around it.
  • Stripping words like "not" or "with" destroys clinical meaning. A query like "is the patient not responding to metformin?" becomes "patient responding metformin" when stopwords are removed, completely reversing the clinician's intent.

BM25 Already Handles Common Words

  • The sparse retrieval side of QueryStore uses BM25, which naturally applies Inverse Document Frequency (IDF).
  • IDF automatically downweighs common words mathematically based on their frequency in the clinical corpus. Manual stopword lists are redundant and carry the risk of stripping clinically relevant context.

Fixing the Multilingual Contradiction

  • QueryStore's Architecture Decision Record (ADR 8) explicitly chose multilingual-e5 to remove English bias and support 100+ languages natively.
  • However, the previous preprocessing step relied on a 260-word English-only stopword list. This caused non-English queries to fail or act unpredictably. Removing the English stopword list restores true multilingual capability to the platform.
