QueryStore: a quick way to read and inspect records

Its most useful lesson is to separate reading from changing the medical record. A read-only view can serve both a reviewer and a model, with each entry still linked to its source.

WITH AN OPENMRS TEST SOURCE

Try the existing read interface

QueryStore can return a patient’s indexed entries as readable text with source identifiers and clinical dates. It supports full-chart pages and searches. The harness could capture a view and pass selected entries to Hub.

Use the complete available view as the first comparison. Search can be a later experiment.

See the verified interface →
WITH WAHS EXAMPLE EXPORTS

Apply the approach first

A simple file reader can expose entries with their source, date and original content. That may be enough for a small pilot.

The current QueryStore module is built for OpenMRS. Support for arbitrary WAHS FHIR exports would need to be designed and tested.

Keep the limits visible. A searchable copy can be incomplete or out of date. Version markers help detect changes; a result count does not prove completeness. Patient access controls need their own design. QueryStore also has maintenance operations, so the pilot should expose only the intended reading functions. Its index repair is different from reconciling clinical meaning.
03 / HUMAN REVIEW WORKFLOW

Review, decide, then review again

Use the same principle as Catalyst: people inspect a suggestion before choosing an action. Keep the original records and a history of decisions.

Proposed workflow · to explore with WAHS
  1. 01

    Compare

    See the relevant source entries together.

  2. 02

    Propose

    Explain the conflict and suggest a next action.

  3. 03

    Review

    Accept, edit, reject, comment or defer.

  4. 04

    Apply

    Save the chosen change and its decision history.

  5. 05

    Preview and share

    Check the combined view and choose what to share.

Repeat as new information arrives. An unresolved disagreement remains a valid result. The first pilot tests comparison and review; editing and export are later possibilities.

DESIGN REQUIREMENTS TO AGREE

Keep the person in control

  • Make the proposed change and affected fields visible.
  • Retain accept, edit, reject and defer as distinct decisions.
  • Keep the original entry and the version the person reviewed.
  • Ask for a fresh review if relevant information changes.
  • Preserve user input when a save fails.
  • Preview sharing separately from editing.

04 / RESEARCH OPPORTUNITIES

Learn from the review process

WAHS offers a practical setting for questions already present in the research programme. The contribution could be a method, a set of cases or evidence about a design choice.

Can AI make fragmented records easier to review while preserving their meaning and people’s control?

A useful pilot can inform this question. A publishable study would also need a literature review, a study plan and suitable participants.

Which information should the model see?Apply our experience with context and retrievalMethod transfer

Try: Give the model all entries in a small case. Compare that with selected entries only when size becomes a problem. QueryStore can support a search comparison for OpenMRS sources.

Learn: Whether selection hides a relevant disagreement, and how much time and input space each approach uses.

Research return: Cases that expose the trade-off between a complete view and a focused view.

Related research →
When do suggestions help?Apply our approach to checking individual decisionsMethod transfer

Try: Start with conflict flags. Later compare these with proposed resolutions. Have reviewers explain when a difference is legitimate and when it needs attention.

Learn: Missed disagreements, incorrect combinations, useful corrections and remaining uncertainty.

Research return: A review method that distinguishes computer checks, patient knowledge and clinical judgment.

Related research →
Does the application honor people’s decisions?Adapt the review-before-action approachDesign transfer

Try: Reject a suggestion, correct an entry or change the sharing purpose. Check what appears in the next draft.

Learn: Whether rejected or outdated suggestions reappear, whether edits survive a failure, and whether unresolved items stay visible.

Research return: Repeatable examples for studying control over AI-assisted work.

Related research →
Do people understand sources and AI involvement?Study explanation and review labelsStudy opportunity

Try: Ask readers to identify the source, what AI changed and who reviewed it, using a concise display with expandable details.

Learn: What readers interpret correctly, where they become too confident, and which wording helps them correct a mistake.

Research return: Evidence about an understandable way to display AI history. A label alone does not establish understanding.

Design research →
What can small local models do well?Reuse the testing approach and, where useful, the toolsTool and method transfer

Try: After the first workflow is useful, run the same reviewed cases on a second model with the same instructions and inputs.

Learn: Which cases succeed, when a model admits uncertainty, and the time and computing resources needed.

Research return: Task-specific evidence for model choice. A small demonstration cannot establish deployment readiness.

05 / A SMALL FIRST PILOT

Start with a read-only review bench

A small experiment: can people find and understand disagreements more easily when sources and AI suggestions are shown together?

Proposed starting point

One kind of record. A few examples. A human review.

Begin with fictional medication-history examples and conflict flags. Preserve all originals. Record reviewer decisions separately. Use the existing harness and Hub only for the parts that save work.

Where QueryStore fits →
1
No code required

Agree the examples and expected decisions

In a short working session, review a handful of fictional cases: an exact duplicate, a real change over time, a disagreement, a missing date and a patient correction. Write down what should be flagged and what must remain unresolved.

What we should have afterward

A small case pack and a review guide, including examples the model will not see while instructions are being refined. Name a WAHS workflow reviewer and a clinical reviewer for the questions that need clinical judgment.

2
Small amount of implementation

Run the cases and show sources beside suggestions

A small harness runner reads the examples, calls one configured model through Hub and saves the exact input, output and any error. A simple report lets a person inspect the suggested disagreement and its source entries.

How the existing tools would contribute

Record access: Read example files directly, or use QueryStore’s patient-record interface for an OpenMRS test source. Preserve original content and record which version was read.

Med Agent Hub: Use its general model-call endpoint. The runner supplies the task instructions and records its own request and response; Catalyst query profiles are specific to another task.

Evaluation harness: Add WAHS cases and collect run records, source references, errors and reviewer notes using the existing conventions.

New work: The case reader, task instructions, result display and review recording are not implemented by this report.

Verified Hub boundary →
3
Joint review

Decide whether the assistance is useful

Compare the reviewer’s first pass with the model-assisted pass. Inspect each missed disagreement, mistaken flag and correction. Record time spent and where more information was needed.

What determines the next step

Check that every cited entry exists and that originals remain unchanged. Keep errors and reviewer disagreements visible. Agree whether the next step is better examples, a revised prompt, a WAHS data connection or a more interactive review screen.

This is a feasibility exercise. A few cases and a repeated reviewer do not establish a reliable improvement or clinical safety; a later study needs a suitable comparison design.

WHAT WE COULD SHARE

A small, inspectable result

A fictional case pack, model and input versions, source-linked suggestions, reviewer decisions, and a brief “what worked / what needs work” report.

These can improve our tools and research even if WAHS chooses a different implementation.

A SEPARATE QUICK OPPORTUNITY

Display incoming AI history

If a partner already has an example, show whether AI was involved, which entries or fields it touched, and what human review is recorded. Make model information easy to open.

Test a whole-entry label and a label on one field. This can inform the review display without making FHIR export a prerequisite for the first pilot.

Four decisions to make together

Which first task?

The starting suggestion is conflict flags in medication histories. Confirm whether a source-linked summary would be more useful.

Which examples?

Start with fictional cases. Inspect the shape and size of a partner-provided example before planning a WAHS connection.

Who reviews what?

Identify workflow and clinical reviewers. Keep patient statements distinguishable from clinical decisions.

What would justify the next step?

Agree what needs to become easier for reviewers and which errors would require redesign. Set a timebox once the cases and reviewers are available.
