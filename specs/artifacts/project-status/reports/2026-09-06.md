**The central finding**

Both products have substantial working implementations. The confusing part is that the main branches, later development branches, written status, demonstrations, and final acceptance evidence describe different revisions.

ChartSearchAI has implemented its two-provider foundation, but final live proof was left unfinished after the August 6 fixes. Its upstream branches have since moved considerably. Catalyst has a working Spark implementation and an August 27 browser demonstration that the main branches and implementation-plan status do not reflect. The first step is to recover and reconcile those states, then finish the remaining proof and review.

**ChartSearchAI: what is implemented**

The durable product decision is two inference providers behind the same OpenMRS conversation experience. A fresh installation retains bundled inference. A configured Med-Agent Hub provider can be offered alongside it; selecting a different provider starts another conversation. An unavailable provider remains visible as unavailable, with no silent substitution. QueryStore owns shared patient-record selection, while each inference provider owns its prompt construction, model budget, and supported answer processing. See the [approved roadmap](https://github.com/pmanko/clinical-ai-validation-harness/blob/4053352dba3c57a10fcaa41f2f987bd9a14c6d40/specs/artifacts/planning/openmrs-dual-provider-parity-roadmap.md#L16) and [shared contract](https://github.com/pmanko/clinical-ai-validation-harness/blob/4053352dba3c57a10fcaa41f2f987bd9a14c6d40/specs/artifacts/planning/openmrs-dual-provider-conformance-contract.md#L69).

Implemented behavior includes provider discovery, conversation storage/reload, cancellation and preemption, shared context selection, completeness/freshness metadata, protected allergy and condition context, explicit context-overflow handling, answer validation and citation display, and visible limitations of medication-safety information. These are substantial product features with source tests and earlier real-path demonstrations.
